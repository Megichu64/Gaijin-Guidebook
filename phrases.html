Quick Language Resources for Travelers

You don't need to be fluent to visit Japan, but a few key resources will make your trip much smoother!

Essential Apps

Duolingo

Great for daily practice to build and maintain your hiragana and katakana muscle memory before you fly.

Kanji Study

A fantastic free resource for practicing kanji. Excellent for daily use, even if the reminders aren't as aggressive as other apps.

Rocket Languages

Dives deep into the "why" of Japanese grammar and speech nuances. It is a paid resource, but highly effective for conversational understanding.

Google Translate

An absolute essential. The camera and conversation features are lifesavers in a pinch, though be prepared for occasional translation errors!

For the Offline Traveler (Books)

Lonely Planet Phrasebook

Pocket-sized and broken down by practical situations like transit and food. The perfect offline backup when your phone battery dies.

Yubisashi Mini Japan

A highly visual "point and speak" book. Just point to the picture and word to communicate with locals—zero pronunciation pressure.

Essential Phrasebook 📖

Save the phrases you need for your trip.

Want perfect pronunciation?

Robotic voices can be hard to understand. We recommend Forvo, a free dictionary with recordings from real native Japanese speakers.

My Phrase Notebook

    👋 Greetings

    Konnichiwa (こんにちは)
    Hello / Good Afternoon
    Arigatou gozaimasu (ありがとうございます)
    Thank you (Polite)
    Sumimasen (すみません)
    Excuse me / I'm sorry

    🛑 Essentials

    Hai / Iie (はい / いいえ)
    Yes / No
    Toire wa doko desu ka? (トイレはどこですか)
    Where is the toilet?
    Eigo ga wakarimasu ka? (英語がわかりますか)
    Do you understand English?

    🍜 Dining

    Oishii desu (おいしいです)
    It's delicious!
    O-kaikei onegaishimasu (お会計お願いします)
    Check, please.
